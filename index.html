World Happiness Report Analysis

Completed

Research Question

To what extent does economic wealth, measured by GDP per capita, explain national happiness, and what other factors contribute to well-being?

Project Overview

This project investigates whether economic wealth alone can explain differences in national happiness. It compares happiness with GDP per capita and considers social support, freedom, health, generosity and perceptions of corruption.

Main Findings

Positive relationshipHigher GDP per capita is generally associated with higher happiness scores.
Wealth is not sufficientCountries with similar wealth can have substantially different happiness outcomes.
Notable exceptionsCosta Rica performs similarly to wealthier countries, while Singapore and Hong Kong score lower than their economic strength might predict.
Happiness is multidimensionalSocial support, freedom, health and institutional factors also contribute to well-being.

Visualizations

Conclusion

Economic wealth provides an important foundation for happiness, but GDP alone cannot fully explain national well-being. The happiest countries generally perform strongly across several social, health and institutional dimensions.

Limitation

The regression simplifies a complex relationship and may hide regional or non-linear patterns. Overlapping observations can also make individual countries difficult to identify, while standardized heatmap values show relative rather than original differences.
